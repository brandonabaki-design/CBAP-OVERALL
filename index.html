The BACCM™ (Core Concept Model)

The conceptual framework for business analysis. Every task is evaluated through these 6 lenses.

Change

The act of transformation in response to a need.

Example: Migrating from manual spreadsheets to a cloud CRM.

Need

A problem or opportunity to be addressed.

Example: Customer wait times are exceeding 15 minutes, causing churn.

Solution

A specific way of satisfying one or more needs in a context.

Example: A self-service mobile app for customers.

Stakeholder

A group or individual with a relationship to the change, need, or solution.

Example: End-users, Project Sponsor, IT Developers.

Value

The worth, importance, or usefulness of something to a stakeholder.

Example: 20% increase in operational efficiency (Tangible) or improved brand reputation (Intangible).

Context

The circumstances that influence, are influenced by, and provide understanding of the change.

Example: New government privacy regulations (GDPR) impacting the industry.

The 6 Knowledge Areas

Select a knowledge area to view its core focus, tasks, and real-world application.

KA 1

Business Analysis Planning & Monitoring

Organizes and coordinates the efforts of business analysts and stakeholders. It defines the "blueprint" for how BA work will be conducted.

Core Tasks
  • Plan Business Analysis Approach (Predictive vs. Adaptive)
  • Plan Stakeholder Engagement
  • Plan Business Analysis Governance
  • Plan Business Analysis Information Management
  • Identify Business Analysis Performance Improvements
Real-World Application

Before gathering requirements for a new HR portal, you decide to use an Agile (adaptive) approach, set up a Jira board for tracking (Info Management), and establish a weekly meeting with the HR Director to approve user stories (Governance).

KA 2

Elicitation & Collaboration

Describes the tasks that BAs perform to obtain information from stakeholders and confirm the results. It's about drawing out info, not just "gathering" it.

Core Tasks
  • Prepare for Elicitation
  • Conduct Elicitation (Interviews, Workshops, Observation)
  • Confirm Elicitation Results
  • Communicate Business Analysis Information
  • Manage Stakeholder Collaboration
Real-World Application

You prepare a questionnaire, host a focus group with end-users to understand their pain points with the current software, and afterward, send out meeting minutes to ensure you captured their needs correctly (Confirmation).

KA 3

Requirements Life Cycle Management

Ensures that business, stakeholder, and solution requirements and designs are aligned to one another and that the solution implements them. It manages requirements from inception to retirement.

Core Tasks
  • Trace Requirements (Forward and Backward)
  • Maintain Requirements (Keep them reusable)
  • Prioritize Requirements (MoSCoW, Timeboxing)
  • Assess Requirements Changes
  • Approve Requirements
Real-World Application

A stakeholder requests a new "chatbot" feature halfway through development. You trace this back to the business need, assess the impact on the budget and timeline, and present it to the sponsor for approval or rejection.

KA 4

Strategy Analysis

Focuses on how to identify the business need, address that need, and align the resulting strategy for the change with higher- and lower-level strategies.

Core Tasks
  • Analyze Current State ("As-Is")
  • Define Future State ("To-Be")
  • Assess Risks
  • Define Change Strategy (How to get from As-Is to To-Be)
Real-World Application

Mapping out a company's current manual supply chain process, envisioning an automated future state, identifying the risk of staff resistance, and proposing a phased software rollout.

KA 5

Requirements Analysis & Design Definition

Structures and organizes requirements discovered during elicitation activities, specifies and models requirements and designs, and validates and verifies information.

Core Tasks
  • Specify and Model Requirements (UML, Use Cases)
  • Verify Requirements (Check for quality/correctness)
  • Validate Requirements (Check for value/business alignment)
  • Define Requirements Architecture
  • Define Design Options
  • Analyze Potential Value and Recommend Solution
Real-World Application

Taking raw interview notes and creating structured Process Flow Diagrams, writing clear User Stories, verifying they meet quality standards (e.g., INVEST criteria), and proposing three different vendor software packages as design options.

KA 6

Solution Evaluation

Assesses the performance of and value delivered by a solution in use by the enterprise, and recommends removal of barriers or constraints that prevent full value realization.

Core Tasks
  • Measure Solution Performance
  • Analyze Performance Measures
  • Assess Solution Limitations
  • Assess Enterprise Limitations
  • Recommend Actions to Increase Solution Value
Real-World Application

Three months after launching a new E-commerce site, you measure the cart abandonment rate. You realize the solution works fine, but users are dropping off due to high shipping costs (an enterprise limitation), and recommend a free shipping promotion.

IIBA® Exam Simulator

Test your knowledge with these past-exam style questions covering concepts from BABOK v3. Click the options to verify your answer.

1. According to the BACCM™, which concept represents the circumstances that influence, are influenced by, and provide understanding of the change?

Core Concepts
Rationale: In the Business Analysis Core Concept Model (BACCM), Context is explicitly defined as the circumstances that influence, are influenced by, and provide understanding of the change.

2. Which knowledge area contains the task "Prioritize Requirements"?

Knowledge Areas
Rationale: Prioritize Requirements is a task within the Requirements Life Cycle Management knowledge area, dealing with the ongoing administration of requirements to ensure the most important ones are addressed first.

3. You are a BA evaluating a newly implemented CRM system. You discover that while the system works perfectly, sales reps are not using it because they don't understand the interface. What have you identified?

Application
Rationale: This falls under "Assess Enterprise Limitations" (Solution Evaluation KA). The solution itself operates as designed (no solution limitation), but factors outside the system (lack of training/culture of the enterprise) are preventing value realization.

4. The difference between verifying a requirement and validating a requirement is that:

Definitions
Rationale: Verification (Quality check - "Did we build the requirements right?") vs. Validation (Value check - "Did we build the right requirements?"). Both are tasks in the Requirements Analysis and Design Definition KA.
