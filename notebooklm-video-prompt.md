## ▶ Main prompt (paste this)

Create a friendly, step-by-step tutorial video that teaches IB Diploma Programme students at our school how to use our "Topic Quest" tool to explore an Extended Essay (EE) topic. The audience is 16–18 year-old IB students who are just starting their EE. Keep it warm, clear, encouraging and concise (about 3–4 minutes), in plain language, and briefly define any IB terms.

Teach the whole journey as five clear steps:

1. PLAY THE GAME. Students open the Topic Quest game at https://brandonabaki-design.github.io/CBAP-OVERALL/ (the link and name may change — tell students to use the current link their teacher shares). They walk around the library and talk to every mentor, answering honestly. Stress that the open-ended mentors — the Dreamer, the Tinkerer, the Counsellor and the Realist — matter most: students should write real, specific answers about what genuinely fascinates them, their hobbies and experiences, the subjects they enjoy, and the kind of work they'd rather avoid. The more honest and detailed they are, the better their topic ideas will be.

2. COPY THE PROMPT. When they finish, the game writes a personalised prompt that summarises their interests and results. They click "Copy my prompt".

3. PASTE INTO NOTEBOOKLM. They paste that prompt into this NotebookLM chat as their first message and send it.

4. CHAT TO EXPLORE A TOPIC. Explain that the coach does not just hand over an answer — it will first ask a few follow-up questions to dig deeper into their interests and hobbies, then suggest several topic ideas that connect to what they care about. Make two things very clear:
   - The coach suggests TOPICS only. It will NOT write a research question for them — turning a topic into a research question is something they do later with their supervisor.
   - It only suggests topics in subjects our school actually offers.

5. SHARE WITH YOUR SUPERVISOR. They take their favourite one or two topics to their EE supervisor, who helps them shape a research question and approve the topic. The student's own thinking and the supervisor lead the way.

Weave in these important points across the video:

- SUBJECTS WE OFFER (the coach stays within these): Biology, Chemistry, Computer science, Physics, Mathematics, Business management, Digital society, Economics, Global politics, Psychology, Language A English (literature or language & literature), Language B Arabic, Visual arts, and the interdisciplinary "World Studies" pathway combining any two of these.

- SUBJECTS WE DO NOT OFFER (the coach will never suggest these, so students shouldn't expect them): Language B in any language other than Arabic; classical languages; Geography; History; Philosophy; Social and cultural anthropology; World religions; Design technology; Sports, exercise and health science; Dance; Film; Music; Theatre; Environmental systems and societies; and Literature and performance. If a student's interest only fits one of these, the coach will help them find a related angle inside a subject we do offer.

- THE HANDBOOK: the subjects and example topics behind this tool come from the IB Extended Essay Guide (first assessment 2027), which is one of the sources in this notebook. Encourage students to open it to see real examples of what strong EE topics look like in each subject.

- THE AI GUIDELINES (also a source in this notebook — "Evaluating 13 scenarios of AI in student coursework"): AI is a tool to help you learn and explore, not to do your work for you. Your Extended Essay must be authentically your own. Be transparent — tell your supervisor you used this tool. Keep a copy of the prompt you used, the date, and a transcript of the chat, and hold on to your notes and drafts so you can show how your thinking developed. Remind students that the coach will not write their research question, essay, paragraphs or reflection — that thinking has to be theirs.

End with a short, motivating wrap-up: Topic Quest helps you start from what genuinely interests you, but your own curiosity, your supervisor, and the IB guidelines lead the way.

Please avoid: making it sound like AI writes the EE for them; suggesting any subject we don't offer; or giving an example research question.
